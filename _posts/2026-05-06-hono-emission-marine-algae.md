Our paper **"HONO emission from marine algae"** was published in *Science Advances*.

Nitrous acid (HONO) is an important precursor of hydroxyl radicals (OH), which are central to atmospheric oxidation chemistry. However, the sources of HONO, especially during daytime and in coastal or marine-influenced environments, remain uncertain.

In this work, we investigated **marine algae as a previously unrecognized source of atmospheric HONO**. The study connects marine biological materials with reactive nitrogen chemistry and evaluates the potential influence of algae-derived HONO on coastal atmospheric oxidation capacity.

This work is closely related to my broader research interests in:

- coastal and marine atmospheric chemistry;
- HONO sources and budgets;
- reactive nitrogen cycling;
- atmospheric oxidation capacity.

For me, this study is especially meaningful because it links controlled experimental evidence with larger atmospheric implications. It also extends my earlier research on coastal HONO budgets and motivates further investigation of marine biological sources of reactive nitrogen.
