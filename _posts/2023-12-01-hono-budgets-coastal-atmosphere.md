Our paper **"Nitrous acid budgets in the coastal atmosphere: potential daytime marine sources"** was published in *Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics*.

Coastal atmosphere is a chemically complex region where continental pollution, marine air masses, photochemistry, and surface processes interact. HONO chemistry in this environment is particularly interesting because observed daytime HONO is often difficult to explain using known sources alone.

In this work, we investigated HONO budgets in the coastal atmosphere using field observations and chemical box modelling. The study focused on evaluating daytime HONO sources and examining whether marine-related processes may contribute to the observed HONO levels.

This paper helped shape my current research direction. It provided an important starting point for my later work on marine HONO sources, including HONO emission from marine algae.

From this study, I became more interested in combining:

- field measurement data;
- chemical box modelling;
- coastal atmospheric processes;
- reactive nitrogen chemistry.
