Research

My research focuses on atmospheric nitrous acid (HONO), with particular interests in its sources, budgets, and impacts on atmospheric oxidation chemistry in coastal and marine environments.

Research Overview

HONO is an important precursor of hydroxyl radicals (OH), which control the oxidation capacity of the lower atmosphere. However, HONO sources and budgets remain uncertain in many atmospheric environments, especially during daytime and in coastal or marine-influenced regions.

My first-author and co-first-author work investigates HONO from three connected perspectives: field budgets in the coastal atmosphere, HONO behavior across contrasting environments in North China, and previously unrecognized marine algal sources.

1. HONO Budgets in the Coastal Atmosphere

Coastal atmosphere is influenced by both continental pollution and marine processes. In this work, we examined HONO budgets in the coastal atmosphere using field observations and chemical box modelling, with a focus on understanding daytime HONO sources and their potential marine contribution.

Paper: Zhong, X. et al. Nitrous acid budgets in the coastal atmosphere: potential daytime marine sources. Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics, 2023.

2. Atmospheric HONO in Contrasting Environments

HONO chemistry can vary substantially among different atmospheric environments. This work investigates atmospheric nitrous acid in contrasting environments in North China, helping to clarify how HONO sources, budgets, and atmospheric roles change under different pollution and environmental conditions.

Paper: Zhong, X. et al. Atmospheric Nitrous Acid in Contrasting Environments in North China. Environmental Science & Technology, 2026.

Read the blog post

3. HONO Emission from Marine Algae

Marine biological processes may provide previously overlooked sources of reactive nitrogen. In this work, we investigated HONO emission from marine algae and evaluated its potential implications for coastal atmospheric oxidation chemistry.

Paper: Shen, H.*, Zhong, X.* et al. HONO emission from marine algae. Science Advances, 2026.

Research Methods

My research combines field measurements, chamber experiments, and chemical box modelling.

Field Experience

I have participated in four field measurement campaigns, including work at a mountain site (Mount Tai, 2021), coastal sites (Qingdao, 2022 and 2024), and an urban site (Dongying, 2022). I have experience with instruments and analytical methods including LOPAP, ToF-CIMS, HCHO analyzer, NO2 analyzer, and ion chromatography.

Experiment Experience

I have participated in two outdoor chamber experiments: an algae emission chamber experiment in Qingdao in 2021 and a comparative chamber experiment of reactive nitrogen measurement instruments in Beijing in 2023. I also designed flow chamber experiments to quantify HONO emission fluxes from different algae under controlled conditions, including light intensity, algal mass, and algal surface area.

Model Project

I developed a HONO chemistry module in the gas-phase Master Chemical Mechanism (MCM) chemical box model to better simulate atmospheric HONO budgets. This mechanism was applied in my research on HONO chemistry.
